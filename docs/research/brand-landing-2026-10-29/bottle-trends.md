# Yagi product family: packaging trend research (2024–2026)

Scope: 2024–2026 fragrance and colour-cosmetics launches and design press, to replace the eight current Yagi bottles with one original family for a white, neutral landing.

## 1. Trend summary

**The bottle is sold as an object.** The 2025 launches covered by design press sell weight and form, not decoration. Lore (Dieline, Dec 2025): pebble proportions, weighty glass, chrome cap, wordmark pressed into the glass, no label. Abel (Oct 2025): frosted sculptural base, rounded white cap, minimal centred text. Lemlan (Oct 2025): square clear body under an oversized sphere cap. Byredo's 2025 refresh thickened the base, lengthened the neck and swapped the glossy black cap for a textured, woodwork-inspired one.

**The cap is the hero.** Caps are bigger and more tactile: Tamburins' mushroom cap (Bottari, 2025), Loewe × Lladró porcelain flowers (2024), Phlur's magnetic ceramic lid, Granhand's solid aluminium cap carrying all the text so the bottle stays blank. Thin metal and glossy plastic caps are on the way out.

**Tactile materials beat shine.** Frosted or etched glass, ceramic, wood texture, soft-touch matte, bio-based caps (Abel, Eauso Vert). Colour comes from tinted-glass gradients (Loewe Solo Elixir, 2025: burnt-umber gradient, wenge cap) or from juice seen through thick clear glass.

**A range is one form in several colours.** Glossier You Doux and Rêve (2024) reuse one bottle in ivory and berry-plum; Loewe colour-codes one block bottle. Prada Paradoxe and Marc Jacobs Daisy Wild build refillability into the form.

**Colour cosmetics are chubby, pocketable and translucent.** Rhode plays with scale (chubby Pocket Blush in upcycled plastic, Lip Cases); Fwee has bubble pudding and jelly pots; YSL Loveshine uses heavy metal; Eme pairs frosted lip-oil tubes with tinted tops. Cosmetics Business expects loud Gen Z packaging to fade in 2026 in favour of tactile matte and embossed surfaces.

**Labels are restrained.** One small mark, embossed or etched; the rest of the text moves to the cap or base. Paper labels survive only as established brand codes (Le Labo, Replica, Diptyque), so a new brand should not borrow them.

**Why this photographs well on white.** Thick bases throw caustics and coloured shadows onto the sweep, frosting gives soft gradients instead of hard reflections, tinted juice glows when backlit, matte caps hold their shape under soft light, and soft silhouettes read at thumbnail size. Mirror chrome and faceted crystal reflect the studio and break into busy highlights, which is where AI renders look cheapest.

**What reads dated in 2026** (each maps onto the current set):
- faceted crystal cubes and gem cuts, amber with brass (2010s luxury)
- glass-sphere stoppers, ribbons, bows (boudoir vintage)
- charred-wood and travertine caps on smoked glass (2019–2022 niche cliché)
- black lacquer with mother-of-pearl, pressed botanicals in glass, cork
- polished chrome on generic cylinders and teardrops (stock-bottle look)
- a different shape and material per product, which reads as a sample shelf

## 2. Family rule

1. **Silhouette.** Soft geometry only. Every edge radius is at least 18% of the shorter side. No facets, points or teardrops.
2. **Glass.** Thick walls and a heavy base. On fragrance the solid base is a quarter of the glass height, and the inner cavity has rounded corners so the liquid seems to float.
3. **The Yagi pebble cap.** Every closure uses the same form: a slightly flattened, domed pebble, a soft superellipse in plan with gently convex sides, no visible seam and no knurling.
   - Material: soft-touch matte bio-resin with a fine stone grain.
   - Colour: tone-on-tone with the vessel, one step deeper.
   - Size: on fragrance, about 50% of the glass height (oversized). On slender vessels (height more than 2.5× width), about 30% of total length.
4. **No visible metal.** The pump, neck and collar hide under the cap. The roll-on ball is the only metal.
5. **One gloss, one matte.** Glossy glass, or a glossy product (oil, balm, bullet), always sits against a matte cap or matte body. Never all-gloss.
6. **Mark.** Only the Yagi script, in one colour: a frosted acid-etch on glass or a tonal deboss on opaque parts.
   - On bottles: 38% of the front-face width, centred at 55% of the glass height, just above the heavy base.
   - On slender vessels: along the long axis, 40% of body length.
   - Name and volume go on the base, in tonal 5–6 pt uppercase grotesk. Nothing else goes on the front.
7. **Palette.** A warm-neutral backbone (milk, oat, tea, smoke) plus one muted accent per product, all at similar saturation. Red is the one loud exception.

## 4. Staying clear of trade dress

Keep out: thumbprint indents (Glossier You), perfect-sphere caps (Lemlan), clear bubble lids (Fwee), round glass with a cylinder cap (Byredo), apothecary bottles with typed or stitched labels (Le Labo, Replica), oval medallion labels (Diptyque), triangles (Prada), gem facets (Kayali), figurative toppers (Daisy, Loewe × Lladró, Tamburins), blank glass with text on the cap (Granhand), phone-case carriers (Rhode).

The Yagi identity is the combination: tone-on-tone matte pebble cap, heavy-base glass, etched script at a fixed height. No single brand owns it.
