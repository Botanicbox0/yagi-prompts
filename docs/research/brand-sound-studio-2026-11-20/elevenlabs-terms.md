## Music

1. **Commercial use by plan (Exhibit A).** Free through "Enterprise Music Lite": "All online and offline commercial use permitted, except film, TV, radio, and Studio Games". Only "Enterprise Music" has "All online and offline commercial use permitted". Online paid ads and social posts are inside self-serve media rights; there is no ad-budget cap; TV and radio commercials are excluded.
2. **Who may be on which plan.** Free, Starter, Creator, Pro: "For Individual Use Only". Scale: "Individuals, or entities or organizations with fewer than 10 employees". Business: "…fewer than 50 employees". Enterprise: no limit. API access: "No" on Free, "Yes" on the rest; docs: "The Music API is only available to paid users."
3. **Two pages conflict with Exhibit A.** The product FAQ at elevenlabs.io/creative says: "Eleven Music requires an additional license for marketing campaigns, advertising, film, TV, games, and enterprise distribution." The archived first version of the music terms (14 August 2025) barred self-serve plans from advertisements. The current Exhibit A (09 October 2026) is the later, contractual text, but the conflict should be settled in writing.
4. **Resale and platforms.** Music Terms §4.3: "You must not engage in, or permit any third party to engage in, Model Resale, including offering any Music Model alongside third-party models, unless ElevenLabs has expressly authorized that activity in writing." "Model Resale" "means any resale, repackaging, redistribution, aggregation, or other making available of Music Models through your or any third-party platform or application". Exhibit A: Model Resale "Prohibited" on every self-serve plan, "Custom" on Enterprise.
   - Carve-out: "an entity does not become a Reseller solely by generating and distributing Output through its own product, provided it does not make the Music Models or API access to them available to any third party".
   - API terms Exhibit 3: "Unless you are an Authorized Reseller, you shall not resell, repackage, redistribute, sublicense, or otherwise make API access to the Music Service available to any third party."
   - OEM Terms §2B: "if you are a Customer accessing the Services under a Free, Starter, Creator, or Pro tier plan, you are expressly prohibited from Making Available the Services or any Bundled Service". Scale and above need an End User Agreement in which "ElevenLabs is a third-party beneficiary of Customer's agreement with End Users".
   - Prohibited Use Policy §12 bans "Making our Services or Output available to your own end users or other third parties on terms more permissive than those that apply to you".
   - Music Library (prohibited on self-serve): "any collection of Output, in whole or in part, made available to third parties for those third parties to use, license, download, or incorporate into their content, whether or not for payment".
5. **Ownership.** ToS §4(c): "Except as expressly set forth herein, as between you and ElevenLabs, you retain all rights in and to your Output." The vendor keeps a perpetual, sublicensable licence to customer content "to provide the Services (including the trust and safety features therein), to improve the Services, and to develop new services and products" (a training opt-out exists in account settings). "the Output generated by you using the Services may not be unique across users". Content ID is not mentioned anywhere; nothing gives labels or publishers a right to claim outputs and nothing excludes it. Streaming distribution: prohibited on Free and Starter, permitted from Creator up.
6. **Attribution and marking.** Free plan: "Required (denote Eleven Music when distributing)". Paid plans: "No Attribution Required". §3.4: "ElevenLabs may mark Output as AI-generated but does not warrant that any Output carries a watermark or provenance signal". Use policy §10: "You must not remove, alter, or conceal ElevenLabs watermarks, metadata or other provenance signals".
7. **Indemnity.** None on self-serve; the ToS disclaims "implied warranties of merchantability, fitness for a particular purpose, title, and non-infringement", and the customer indemnifies the vendor (ToS §9; OEM §7 for end-user breaches). An enterprise indemnity appears only as marketing: "provides IP indemnification for audio" (elevenlabs.io/creative); its scope is not public.
8. **Training data.** "Eleven Music is trained on licensed stems and music" (elevenlabs.io/eleven-music-api). Launch post (5 Aug 2025): "Created in collaboration with labels, publishers, and artists, Eleven Music is cleared for nearly all commercial uses". Music v2 post (26 May 2026): "including recent licensing collaborations with Believe". The vendor's own pages do not name Merlin or Kobalt; trade press (TechCrunch, 5 Aug 2025, secondary) reported deals with both.
9. **Prompts.** §2.1 bans "any artist's real name or stage name, whether living or deceased", songwriter names, song and album titles, publisher and label names, and "a substantial or distinctive portion of any song's lyrics". §3.3 bans output that "replicates or mimics the voice, likeness, or identifiable characteristics of any recording artist". API: "the API will return a `bad_prompt` error that contains a suggestion of what prompt you could use instead".
10. **Already-made tracks.** §1.5: on downgrade or termination, "Output remains subject to the plan in effect when that Output was created, and new Output is subject to your then-current plan". §5.2: "An update that materially reduces your rights takes effect 30 days after ElevenLabs notifies you". Help centre: "you will still have a commercial license to use whatever you generated during that subscription forever".
11. **Through an aggregator.** An aggregator "offering any Music Model alongside third-party models" is a Reseller and needs written authorisation. If authorised, ToS §17: "these Terms will act as the end user license agreement". No public list of authorised aggregators exists, and which Exhibit A tier applies to aggregator-generated output is not stated.
12. **Other.** No use of output "to develop, train, fine-tune, or improve any AI model or service"; no music libraries; the user must review output "for clearance before using, publishing, or distributing it" (§3.1). "Worldwide" and "perpetual" are not written for music; there is no territory limit and Korea is not a restricted country.

## Sound effects (where different)

- **Commercial use.** Creative Terms Exhibit 4 §2 allows use "including by incorporating it into films, trailers, advertising, video, games, applications, and other productions" "and for commercial purposes only where your plan permits commercial use". No film/TV/radio carve-out and no employee-count rule. Product page: "As long as you have a paid account, you can use our sound effects royalty-free in commercial projects, including YouTube videos, social media content, and even advertising".
- **Only inside a production.** "You may exercise those rights only in respect of Output combined with other material"; output may not be offered as isolated files "or as part of any sound, sample, effects, or music library or other collection of sounds".
- **Platform use.** No sound-effect-specific resale clause; the OEM Terms (Scale and above, pass-through End User Agreement) and the "terms more permissive" rule apply.
- **Attribution.** Free or signed-out use must credit the vendor; paid use none. Marking rules as for music.
- **Indemnity.** None on self-serve.
- **Training.** Launch post (31 May 2024): "we were able to fine tune our model using the expansive and diverse content from the Shutterstock audio library of licensed tracks".

## Questions that need a written answer from the vendor's sales team

1. Is generation prompted by our customers inside our product "Model Resale", or covered by the "own product" carve-out?
2. Is storing and delivering each customer's own tracks a "Music Library"?
3. Does the employee count (under 10 for Scale, under 50 for Business) look at us only, or at our brand customers too?
4. Which governs advertising: the product FAQ ("additional license for … advertising") or Exhibit A?
5. Are connected-TV placements of an online ad "TV"?
6. What does the enterprise IP indemnity cover, and does it reach our customers?
7. Which aggregators are Authorized Resellers, and which plan tier applies to output made through them?
8. May our customers register outputs with content-identification systems?
9. May a sound effect be delivered to a customer as a file on its own?
